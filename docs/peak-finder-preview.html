TrekSense · Screen preview

Peak Finder, once it's built

A working mockup of the AR view from Blueprint v2 — the skyline and sensors are simulated, but the layout, the label behaviour, the compass HUD, the detail sheet and map mode are exactly what the real feature would show.

Try it: drag across the screen to look around, tap a peak label for its detail card, switch to Map, or tap the amber “compass low” chip to see the calibration flow.

Viewpoint: above Sankri / Juda Ka Talab (≈ 2,750 m), Har Ki Dun valley, Uttarakhand. Peak names, elevations, bearings and distances are real; the ridge silhouette is illustrative.

Compass ribbon
True heading (declination-corrected) with the amber readout locked to the reticle. Scrolls as you pan.
Status chip · left
Where the on-device data is centred and how old it is. Lit green when a fresh prepare is possible; here it's offline, prepared 2 h ago.
Sensor chip · right
Pose confidence. Tap when it reads “low” to run figure-eight calibration.
Peak labels
Placed by bearing (x) and elevation angle (y). Nearest peak sits on top; overlapping labels stack upward with leader lines to the summit.
AR / Map toggle
Map mode is the no-camera fallback: a north-up radial plot with the field-of-view wedge and a peak per dot.

Not final visual design — spacing, type and colour follow the blueprint's system and are open to change.
