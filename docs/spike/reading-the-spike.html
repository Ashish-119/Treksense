Something looks wrong — what is it?

Match what you're seeing to a row. Read left to right: the symptom, the most likely cause, and the fastest way to check or fix it.

SymptomLikely causeDo this
TARGET label never turns green, even aimed right (by a map/real compass) wrong true/magnetic assumption, or a fixed offset from interference Run 3–5 known‑bearing checks; if the winning hypothesis still errs by more than ~5°, you have local interference — move away from metal and retest
Label green, then creeps off target over a few seconds while the phone is still high steady‑state jitter, or a moving magnetic source nearby Check jitter σ — if it's low (<1°) but this still happens, look for someone/something with metal or another phone passing near you
Raw heading jumps tens of degrees in an instant a momentary interference spike, or the source silently fell back from absolute to relative Glance at source and absolute seen right when it happens
Standing in the same spot facing the same landmark gives a different raw heading than 10 minutes ago poor repeatability — hard/soft‑iron distortion where you're standing Walk a few metres away from cars, railings, large metal structures, retest

The top of the screen

What you see before opening the readout panel

Big heading readout e.g. "80° E true"
The computed true heading: smoothed heading, plus declination if the "heading is" selector says magnetic, or the smoothed value directly if it says already‑true. Everything else on the page is measured against this number.
ideal matches a real compass/map within ±5°, updates smoothly, doesn't freeze
bad frozen for several seconds while you're moving = sensor stopped delivering events
Source tag
Which API actually supplied the last heading: iOS's true‑north compass API, the W3C absolute orientation event (normally magnetic‑frame), or a plain relative event with no world anchor at all.
ideal ios:webkitCompassHeading (true) on iPhone · w3c:absolute alpha on Android
bad RELATIVE alpha (unreliable) — heading is drifting from an arbitrary start point, not anchored to the real world
Compass tick‑strip
A protractor centred on your current true heading. The cardinal letters (N/E/S/W) should sit where those directions actually are.
ideal "N" tick roughly toward where you independently know north to be
TARGET label
Marks the bearing you typed into "true bearing°", placed by (target − true heading) ÷ hFOV. Turns green ("lock") within ±2° of centre.
ideal centres and locks green when you physically face that true bearing, verified against a map or real compass

Readout panel · rows 1–8

The heading pipeline

raw heading (mag / true frame)
The newest heading value straight from the sensor event, before any smoothing. The label switches between "mag frame" and "true frame" depending on the detected source.
ideal changes smoothly and proportionally as you turn; returns to the same value when you return to the same physical direction
bad different values each time you revisit the same direction — that's a repeatability failure, i.e. local interference
smoothed
Raw heading passed through the selected filter (None / EMA / Complementary).
ideal tracks raw closely but visibly steadier on the tick‑strip / label than raw would be
smoothing lag
|smoothed − raw| right now — how far behind the filter currently sits.
ideal small (< 2°) at rest; briefly larger the instant you start panning, then collapses within ~1 s of stopping
watch stays > 5–8° for several seconds after you stop — filter is too heavy (α too low) for this device
event rate
Orientation events per second, measured over the last ~2 s.
ideal ≥ 20 Hz (most iPhones deliver 60 Hz)
bad < 8 Hz — the label will visibly stutter; note the device, it limits how much smoothing helps later
iOS compass accuracy
Apple's own self‑reported uncertainty of the compass reading (webkitCompassAccuracy), in degrees. iOS only — blank elsewhere.
ideal a small number, roughly ≤ 10–15°
bad negative = uncalibrated (do the figure‑8); > 20° = iOS itself doesn't trust this reading
absolute seen
Whether the browser has fired at least one world‑anchored orientation event since the page loaded.
ideal yes
bad NO — every heading shown this session on this device is relative‑frame guesswork; treat all bearings as meaningless until this flips
declination
The correction added to a magnetic‑frame heading to reach true north (Stage 0's coarse regional stand‑in for the real WMM). Shows whether it's currently being applied.
ideal "NOT applied" when source already says (true); "applied" otherwise — confirmed correct by the known‑bearing check, not assumed

Readout panel · rows 9–11

Tilt & motion

pitch (look angle)
How far up/down the phone is tilted from "looking at the horizon" (0°), from beta − 90.
ideal close to 0° while doing bearing checks — phone held up, roughly vertical, pointed at the horizon
watch |pitch| > 20° during a known‑bearing log — heading is still usable, but treat that check as lower‑confidence since it's not how you'd actually hold the phone in AR use
roll
Side‑to‑side tilt (rotation around the phone's forward axis), from gamma.
ideal near 0° — phone held level, not canted sideways
gyro rate z
Current rotation rate around the vertical (yaw) axis, from devicemotion's rotationRate.alpha, in °/s.
ideal near 0 when still; a number that tracks your panning speed while turning
bad "— (no devicemotion)" — this device can't run the Complementary filter; it's EMA‑only for D‑0.1
screen angle
OS‑reported screen rotation, used to correct heading for orientation.
ideal 0° — the spike's maths assumes portrait; anything else means you rotated the phone

Readout panel · rows 12–17

Position & camera

position
Your GPS‑derived latitude/longitude.
ideal stable — doesn't jump while you stand still; jumps of 10–20 m+ at rest mean a poor lock
gps accuracy
The device's own confidence radius for the fix, in metres.
ideal < 20 m outdoors, often < 10 m after a short wait
watch > 50 m — wait longer under open sky before trusting distances computed from it (barely affects heading itself)
gps altitude
Reported elevation above sea level, with its own accuracy figure.
ideal a plausible number for the location, small ± accuracy
watch "null (not provided)" — happens on some Android devices; the real app needs a fallback there
camera
The actual resolution/facing the browser granted for the video stream.
ideal environment (rear) facing, a real non‑zero resolution
camera FOV exposed
Whether the camera API told us the lens's field of view.
ideal (expected) "no" almost everywhere — not a fault, it just means Stage 3's manual FOV calibration is mandatory, not optional

Test 3 output

Jitter test

samples · Hz
How many readings were captured in the 30 s window and at what rate.
watch a low sample count is an event‑rate problem, not a jitter problem — check "event rate" first
steady‑state σ
Standard deviation of headings around the mean, after the first 3 s of settling is excluded. The number that predicts how much the label will shimmer.
ideal < 1°; 1–2.5° is workable with smoothing; > 2.5° means the raw compass on this device is genuinely noisy
steady‑state peak‑to‑peak
The worst single swing away from the mean, in the settled portion only.
ideal < 4°
startup transient (ignored)
The largest deviation in the first 3 s — shown for context, not used in the verdict.
normal a larger number here (sensor/hand settling) is expected and not a red flag by itself
ReminderRun it 2–3 times and keep the worst steady‑state σ — one good run doesn't rule out interference you just happened not to be near.

Test 4 output

Known‑bearing check

errorIfRawIsTrue / errorIfRawIsMagnetic
The two competing hypotheses' error against the true bearing you typed in: how far off the label would be if the raw reading were already true north, versus if it were magnetic and needed the declination correction.
ideal after 3–5 checks in different directions, one hypothesis consistently wins with small error (< 5°) and the other is consistently larger — that tells you the frame
bad both are large and inconsistent across checks — that's not a frame problem, it's interference; the compass itself needs a cleaner spot
